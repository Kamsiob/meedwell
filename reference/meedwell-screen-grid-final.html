Meedwell · final screen grid

The complete design record in one file. Every screen ships in both themes; the light set here shows the corrected ink scale. The mark is flat rustic copper: a coin at rest in a shallow open cradle, the meed held well. The old scrim law is retired. In its place: artwork is always shown complete, never cropped, never faded, never written on. Words live only on theme surface, past a hard edge, and a full-screen viewer waits one tap behind every cover. The single exception is the now-playing wash, a palette-drawn color field clamped so white always passes; it is not the artwork. Gold means money reaching makers and appears nowhere else, deepened on paper to #8A6215 so the label passes AA. The waveform is the music's own portrait, tapped from Meedwell's decoder, never the microphone.

The mark
Meedwell

A rustic copper coin at rest in a shallow copper cradle, flat on near-black. A record in an open sleeve, a coin in a waiting palm, the pan of an honest scale: the earned reward, received and held, the way money should come to rest with the people who made the music. Two shapes an eight-year-old could redraw.

Privacy
The whole sheet. This is all of it.
What Meedwell collects
Nothing. No analytics, no telemetry, no identifiers, nothing that leaves this phone.
Your listening history
One database file, here, powering the forgotten shelf and the waveforms. Erase it any time in Settings.
Network traffic
Exactly one server: Bandcamp's, to stream and sync what you own. That traffic follows their privacy policy. Meedwell doesn't have one, because there's nothing to govern.
Sharing and outside links
Sharing hands a plain Bandcamp link to Android's own share sheet. Links open in your browser. Meedwell fetches nothing along the way.

What's in the file
Listening history, lists and hearts made on this phone, your resume points on long pieces, every setting, and the list of what you downloaded with the paths where it lives. Not the music itself: the audio files are already yours, sitting in Music where any app can see them.

Restore puts all of it back on a new phone or a fresh install. It happens in one shot or not at all, and it tells you plainly if the file holds something this version doesn't understand rather than dropping it quietly.

Two permissions,
both narrow
Asked for here, in plain words, before Android asks
Notifications
Only so the player can appear in the shade and on the lock screen. That is the entire use. Meedwell will never send you a notification about anything else.
Music and audio files
So Meedwell can see the files it downloaded for you, and any music already on the phone. Nothing outside your music folders is read.
Say no to either and Meedwell still plays. You lose the lock screen controls, or the local files, and nothing else. Both can be turned on later in Settings.
